Hunt 1: the link gap

Give your site and up to 20 competitors. The reply ranks every site that links to at least two of them by how many it links to and by authority, leaves out blog hosts, site builders and shorteners, and marks the ones that already link to you as earned.

curl https://webscraper.apexflowlabs.com/v1/links/gap \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $APEX_SCRAPER_API_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"domain": "webscraper.apexflowlabs.com", "competitors": ["firecrawl.dev", "scrapingbee.com", "zenrows.com", "scrapfly.io"], "min_authority": 200}'

Hunt 2: dead pages and the archive

The background job asks the Internet Archive for every page it saw working on a competitor's site, checks each one live, keeps the dead ones, and reads the archived copy so you see what the page said. Write the better version, then tell the sites in who_links_to_the_site.

curl https://webscraper.apexflowlabs.com/v1/links/dead \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $APEX_SCRAPER_API_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"domain": "zenrows.com", "limit": 120, "read_archive": 6}'

Hunt 3: find the exact link

Point it at a site and a target, a domain or one exact page such as a competitor's dead article. It learns what the target is about from its home page, reads the pages most likely to hold the link first, and returns each link with its anchor text and follow status.

curl https://webscraper.apexflowlabs.com/v1/links/find \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $APEX_SCRAPER_API_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"target": "scrapingbee.com", "site": "pagecrawl.io", "max_pages": 15}'

The real result: found on the 15 pages it read.

After the gap: contacts, lost links and history

/v1/links/contacts reads a linking site's own pages and returns the public contact emails, contact and "write for us" pages and social profiles it publishes. /v1/links/changes lists the strong sites that started or stopped linking to a domain between the last two quarterly releases: lost links are the easiest to win back. /v1/links/history shows a domain's referring domains and authority every quarter since 2018.

What it is, plainly

The index is domain-level: it knows which sites link to which, not every page and anchor. It lists links from the web's five million best-connected domains and includes a domain once two of them link to it. Exact pages and anchors come from live reads by the link finder. It is rebuilt from Common Crawl's public web graph, so it reflects the web as last crawled (August to October 2026 for this release), not this morning.

Questions

Where does the link data come from?

From our own link index, built from Common Crawl's public web graph: 135 million domains and 1.8 billion links between them, from the August to October 2026 crawls. We rebuild it when Common Crawl publishes a new graph. Page-level links, anchors and dead pages come from live reads of the web by our own crawler.

Is authority the same as Ahrefs Domain Rating or Moz Domain Authority?

No. It is our own 0 to 1,000 score from a domain's harmonic centrality, a measure of how close it sits to every other site in the link graph. 1,000 is the best-connected site on the web. Compare domains with each other on our scale, not with another vendor's.

Why is my small site not found?

The index lists links from the web's five million best-connected domains and includes a domain once at least two of them link to it. A new or small site often has fewer, which is exactly what the link gap fixes. The SEO checks and the link finder still work on any site.

How does the dead-page hunt work?

It asks the Internet Archive for every page it saw working on a competitor's site, checks each one live, and keeps those that now return 404 or 410, redirect to the home page, or whose domain is gone. It reads the archived copy of the first few so you see what they said, drops template pages that were never real, and lists the strong sites linking to that competitor so you know who to tell about your better version.
